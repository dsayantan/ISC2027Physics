How to use this deck

  • Sit the paper first. These are the answers; reading them before you have attempted the questions tells you only that they look easy.
  • Move with ← and →, or the Next and Prev buttons. s shows or hides the solution, d marks a question done, and the contents bar jumps to a section. On a phone, swipe.
  • The marks beside each step are the marks. They come from the same table as the printed marking scheme, so if your working stops halfway you can see exactly what it was worth.
  • Where a question offers a choice, both alternatives are given, the second under an or rule. In the examination you would answer only one.
  • Read the examiner’s notes. They say what earns partial credit and name the mistake each question is set to catch.
  • Printing lays every slide out in order with the solutions revealed, so it doubles as a printable solution booklet.

Section A · Electrostatics

Q1 (v)1 markElectrostatics

Why must the electrostatic field at the surface of a charged conductor be normal to the surface at every point?

A tangential component would drive a current along the surface; in electrostatic equilibrium no current flows, so the tangential component is zero and the field is wholly normal. Accept any answer making the point that a tangential field would move the free charges.
1
Total for this question 1

Why is manganin preferred to copper for making standard resistance coils?

Manganin has a very low temperature coefficient of resistance, so its resistance barely changes in use. Accept also: high resistivity, so a short length suffices.

Question 73 marksElectrostatics

Using Gauss' theorem, derive an expression for the electric field intensity due to an infinite plane sheet of charge of surface charge density σ. Hence write down the field just outside a charged conductor.

Gaussian surface: a cylinder of cross-section A piercing the sheet symmetrically. By symmetry E is normal to the sheet and equal on the two flat faces; the flux through the curved surface is zero
1
Φ = 2EA = q/ε0 = σA/ε0
1
E = σ/2ε0, independent of distance; just outside a charged conductor E = σ/ε0
1
Total for this question 3
Examiner’s noteThe third mark is split: ½ for σ/2ε0 and ½ for the conductor result σ/ε0. A candidate who omits the factor 2 in the flux, and so obtains σ/ε0 for the sheet, loses the second mark but may still earn the third if the conductor case is right.

Section C · Current Electricity

Question 83 marksCurrent Electricity

State Kirchhoff's two laws for an electrical network, and use them to obtain the condition of balance of a Wheatstone bridge.

or

Resistors of 6·0 Ω and 3·0 Ω are connected in parallel across a battery of emf 12 V and internal resistance 1·0 Ω. Calculate the current drawn from the battery and the current in each resistor.

Junction rule: the algebraic sum of the currents at a junction is zero (conservation of charge). Loop rule: the algebraic sum of the potential differences round any closed loop is zero (conservation of energy)
1
At balance no current flows through the galvanometer, so I1 passes through P and Q, and I2 through R and S; applying the loop rule to the two halves gives I1P = I2R and I1Q = I2S
1
Dividing: P/Q = R/S
1
Total for this question 3
Examiner’s noteBoth laws are needed for the first mark, ½ each. The balance condition quoted without the no-galvanometer-current argument scores 1 of the last 2.
the alternative
Parallel pair: (6)(3)/9 = 2·0 Ω; total resistance 2·0 + 1·0 = 3·0 Ω
1
I = ε/Rtotal = 12/3·0 = 4·0 A; p.d. across the pair = (4·0)(2·0) = 8·0 V
1
I6 = 8·0/6·0 = 1·3 A and I3 = 8·0/3·0 = 2·7 A
1
Total for this question 3
Examiner’s noteAccept 1·33 A and 2·67 A. A candidate who forgets the internal resistance gets I = 6·0 A and loses the second and third marks; if the method for splitting the current is then correct, award ½ of the third.

Question 103 marksMagnetic Effect of Current and Magnetism

Derive an expression for the force per unit length between two long, straight, parallel conductors carrying currents I1 and I2, separated by a distance r. Hence define the ampere.

The first wire produces at the second a field B1 = μ0I1/2πr, perpendicular to it
1
A length l of the second wire feels F = B1I2l, so F/l = μ0I1I2/2πr — attractive for parallel currents
1
Definition: one ampere is that steady current which, in each of two infinitely long parallel conductors of negligible cross-section 1 m apart in vacuum, produces a force of 2 × 10−7 N per metre of length
1
Total for this question 3
Examiner’s noteThe direction (attractive / repulsive) is worth ½ of the second mark. In the definition, all of ‘1 m apart’, ‘in vacuum’ and the figure 2 × 10−7 N m−1 are required for the full mark; omitting one costs ½.

Section D · Electrostatics

Question 115 marksElectrostatics

(a) Derive an expression for the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor when a dielectric slab of thickness t and dielectric constant K is introduced between its plates, the plate separation being d (t < d). [3]

(b) A parallel plate air capacitor has a capacitance of 8·0 pF. Its plate separation is halved and the space between the plates is completely filled with a medium of dielectric constant 5·0. Calculate the new capacitance. [2]

or

(a) Derive an expression for the potential energy of an electric dipole of moment p placed at an angle θ to a uniform electric field E, and state the positions of stable and unstable equilibrium. [3]

(b) A dipole of moment 2·0 × 10−9 C m is held in a uniform field of 3·0 × 105 N C−1. Calculate the work done in rotating it from the position of stable equilibrium through 180°. [2]

(a) The field is E0 = σ/ε0 across the air gap of thickness (d−t) and is reduced to E0/K inside the slab
1
V = E0(d−t) + (E0/K)t = E0[(d−t) + t/K]
1
With C = Q/V and E0 = Q/ε0A: C = ε0A/[d − t(1 − 1/K)]
1
(b) Halving d doubles C, and filling with K = 5·0 multiplies it by five: C′ = 2KC
1
C′ = (2)(5·0)(8·0) = 80 pF
1
Total for this question 5
Examiner’s noteIn (a), a candidate who checks the result by putting t = d or t = 0 has earned nothing extra but has done the right thing. In (b), award the fourth mark for recognising C ∝ K/d even if the arithmetic then slips.
the alternative
(a) The torque holding the dipole at θ is τ = pE sin θ, so dW = pE sin θ dθ
1
Integrating from the reference position θ = 90°, where U is taken as zero: U = −pE cos θ = −p·E
1
U is least (−pE) at θ = 0 — stable; greatest (+pE) at θ = 180° — unstable
1
(b) W = U180° − U0° = pE − (−pE) = 2pE
1
W = 2(2·0 × 10−9)(3·0 × 105) = 1·2 × 10−3 J
1
Total for this question 5
Examiner’s noteThe third mark is ½ for each equilibrium position, and requires the words stable and unstable. In (b) a candidate who writes W = pE (forgetting the factor 2) loses the fourth mark but keeps the fifth if the arithmetic matches their own expression.

Section D · Magnetic Effect of Current and Magnetism

Question 125 marksMagnetic Effect of Current and Magnetism

(a) Using Ampère's circuital law, derive an expression for the magnetic flux density inside a long straight solenoid carrying a current I, having n turns per unit length. [3]

(b) A solenoid 50 cm long has 500 turns and carries a current of 2·0 A. Calculate the magnetic flux density near its centre. [2]

or

(a) Derive an expression for the torque on a rectangular coil of N turns and area A, carrying a current I, placed in a uniform magnetic field B. [3]

(b) A coil of 50 turns and area 2·0 × 10−2 m2 carries a current of 3·0 A in a uniform field of 0·10 T, with the plane of the coil parallel to the field. Calculate the torque on it. [2]

(a) Ampèrian loop: a rectangle with one long side of length L inside the solenoid, parallel to the axis, the opposite side well outside
1
Outside a long solenoid the field is negligible, and on the two short sides B ⊥ dl, so only the inner side contributes: ∮B·dl = BL
1
Current threading the loop = nLI, so BL = μ0nLI, giving B = μ0nI — independent of position, i.e. the field inside is uniform
1
(b) n = 500/0·50 = 1000 turns m−1
1
B = (4π × 10−7)(1000)(2·0) = 2·5 × 10−3 T
1
Total for this question 5
Examiner’s noteA candidate who uses N = 500 in place of n and obtains 1·3 × 100 T loses the fourth mark; the fifth is then available only if the substitution is consistent with their own n. Accept 2·51 × 10−3 T or 2·5 mT.
the alternative
(a) The two sides of length l perpendicular to B each carry a force F = BIl; they are equal, opposite and not collinear, so they form a couple, while the forces on the other two sides are collinear and cancel
1
Their perpendicular separation is b sin θ, where θ is the angle between the normal to the coil and B, so τ = BIl·b sin θ = BIA sin θ
1
For N turns τ = NIAB sin θ, or τ = m × B with m = NIA
1
(b) The plane of the coil is parallel to B, so the normal is perpendicular to it: θ = 90° and sin θ = 1 — the position of maximum torque
1
τ = (50)(3·0)(2·0 × 10−2)(0·10) = 0·30 N m
1
Total for this question 5
Examiner’s noteThe fourth mark is for recognising θ = 90° from ‘plane parallel to the field’. This is the standard trap: a candidate who reads θ = 0 and answers zero scores the three derivation marks and nothing more.
